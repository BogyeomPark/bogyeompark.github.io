Demos / Self-disclosing chatbot

The chatbot goes first

Asking a student to describe their academic stress gets you a short answer. In our CHI EA 2025 study we built two chatbots that asked exactly the same questions, and gave one of them something to say about itself first. This is that one. It tells you it cannot sleep before it asks whether you can.

A demonstration, not a screening tool — nothing here assesses anyone. Everything you type stays in your browser and is never sent anywhere.

Narrated, just under a minute: the same conversation you can have above, with the disclosures marked as they arrive and the scoring at the end.

What this measures

The bot works through the SISCO Inventory of Academic Stress — its causes, its physical, psychological and behavioural symptoms, and the ways people cope — but never as a questionnaire. Each list arrives only after the bot has said which item on it applies to itself.

  • The disclosures are fixed, not generated. Every message marked in the conversation is written out verbatim in the study’s protocol, which is why this demo needs no model behind it. What a model supplied in the study was the thread between them.
  • The control asked the same questions. The non-self-disclosing condition ran the identical inventory with the disclosures removed. That single difference is the whole independent variable.
  • The assessment is assembled, not answered. A second pass scored each transcript against the inventory rather than asking anyone to fill it in. The closing screen here reads your own words back the same way.

What the study found

Forty university students were assigned to one of the two chatbots. Students who talked to the self-disclosing version identified their stressors more clearly and planned further ahead than students who talked to the version that only asked. An earlier round with twenty students, reported at HCI Korea 2025, won a Best Paper Award.
