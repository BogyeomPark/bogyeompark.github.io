Counterfactual vs. Prefactual: Two Narrative AIs Improve Causability for Health Data by Different Mechanisms

Hyobin Park (now Bogyeom Park) and Kyoungwon Seo

HCI Korea 2023 · Pages 828–835

Open PDF ↗Publisher ↗
On this pageOverviewMethodKey FindingsContributionCitation

Overview

An MRI report tells a patient what was found, not what to do about it. Narrative AI adds persuasion to a causal explanation so the result can actually change behavior — but a narrative can look backwards (“if you had done X”) or forwards (“if you do X”), and which one serves a patient better was an open question.

Method

Two versions of an MRI report were written, one counterfactual and one prefactual, and shown to 20 participants under a memory-loss scenario. Causability was measured with the seven-item System Causability Scale and compared with a Mann-Whitney U test, followed by semi-structured interviews about how each report was read.

Key Findings

No differenceCausability scores between the counterfactual and prefactual reports; both rated high.
Self-reflectionWhat counterfactual reports set off — participants looked back at past actions and asked how the analysis worked.
Self-improvementWhat prefactual reports set off — participants planned what to change next.

Contribution

  • Two narratives that score the same can work through different mechanisms, which a scale alone would miss.
  • Gives a basis for choosing a narrative by what a patient needs — to understand the past, or to act on the future.
  • Treats causability as something to design for, not only to measure.

Citation
