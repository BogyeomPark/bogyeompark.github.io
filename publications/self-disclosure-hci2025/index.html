The Impact of Self-Disclosing Chatbots for Academic Stress Assessment on Student Self-Reflection

Minyoung Park, Bogyeom Park, and Kyoungwon Seo

HCI Korea 2025 · Pages 560–568

Best Paper Award
Open PDF ↗
On this pageOverviewMethodKey FindingsContributionCitation

Overview

A stress questionnaire only measures what a student can already articulate. Counseling can elicit deeper reflection, but many students lack ready access to it. This study asks whether a chatbot that discloses something of its own can move a student from answering questions to actually reflecting.

Method

Twenty university students were randomly assigned to a self-disclosing or a non-self-disclosing chatbot, both built around the SISCO Academic Stress Inventory. Stress levels were compared to rule out a pre-existing difference, and interviews after the session were analyzed for how each condition shaped reflection.

Key Findings

Reflective inquiryThe disclosing chatbot’s empathetic questions drew deeper answers; the neutral one felt mechanical and students replied without engaging.
Perceived agencyStudents could steer the disclosing conversation toward what mattered to them, where the neutral condition left them answering prompts.
Future planningOnly the disclosing condition led students to leave with concrete plans for handling stress, rather than awareness alone.

Contribution

  • Shows self-disclosure changing the quality of an assessment, not only its pleasantness.
  • Names the three factors — reflective inquiry, perceived agency, future planning — that separated the conditions.
  • Reports the privacy and data-security concerns students raised about disclosure in an educational setting.

Citation

Park, M., Park, B., & Seo, K. (2025). The Impact of Self-Disclosing Chatbots for Academic Stress Assessment on Student Self-Reflection. In Proceedings of HCI Korea 2025, 560–568.
