How Self-Disclosing Chatbots Influence Student Engagement, Assessment Accuracy, and Self-Reflection in Academic Stress Assessment

Minyoung Park, Bogyeom Park, and Kyoungwon Seo

CHI EA ’25 · Article 322 · Pages 1–10 · Published April 25, 2025

Overview

This study examines how chatbot self-disclosure shapes students’ experience of an academic-stress assessment. The study considers engagement, assessment accuracy, and depth of self-reflection together rather than treating the chatbot only as a measurement interface.

Method

Forty university students interacted with either a self-disclosing or non-self-disclosing chatbot. Both chatbots integrated the SISCO Academic Stress Inventory, allowing the study to isolate how self-disclosure influenced the assessment experience.

Key Findings

17.5 minAverage session with the self-disclosing chatbot, against 10.6 minutes without (p < .001).
0.52Assessment accuracy, up from 0.35 without self-disclosure (p < .05).
6.0%Share of the conversation reaching a reflective outcome, up from 2.4%.

Research Contribution

  • Shows that carefully designed chatbot self-disclosure can increase engagement during a sensitive self-assessment.
  • Connects conversational design choices with both assessment accuracy and reflective depth.
  • Offers evidence for designing mental-health and educational assessment chatbots that support more meaningful participation.
